Study prompts

Tested prompts for studying with an AI chat assistant — the same for every chapter.

These prompts are chapter-independent. They assume you have already pasted the chapter’s notation primer into the chat first (copy it from that chapter’s page), so the assistant answers in this book’s symbols and conventions. Copy one, fill in the bracketed parts, and adapt freely.

Each prompt lists a check to run on the reply and a common failure to watch for — AI output is a starting point, not an answer key. New to this? See How to use AI with this book.

Quiz me study

Write 5 questions on this chapter to test my understanding: a mix of
concept and setup questions, not just plug-and-chug. For multiple choice,
use options labeled (a)-(e), make exactly one correct, vary which letter
is correct, and avoid giveaway distractors. Give the answer key separately,
AFTER I answer, not inline.

CHAPTER / TOPIC: <name it>
Check: Verify the keyed answers against the chapter — generated questions can have wrong keys.
Common failure: Models produce malformed multiple choice (all "a", repeated letters) or an inline answer key.

Reconcile notation with another source before

I am reading another source that uses different notation from the primer
above. Map ITS symbols to THIS book's notation, term by term, and flag any
symbol that means something different in the two sources (e.g. sign
conventions, what an overbar means). Do not silently translate.

OTHER SOURCE NOTATION: <paste or describe>
Check: Confirm the work-sign convention specifically — it differs between common thermo texts.
Common failure: Models map symbols by shape and miss that the same symbol carries a different convention.

Explain this in different language during

Explain the following concept in a different way than my textbook does —
a physical picture, an analogy, or a worked micro-example. Keep it
consistent with the notation and conventions in the primer above.

CONCEPT: <paste concept or passage>
Check: If the explanation contradicts the primer's conventions, it is wrong — say so and re-ask.
Common failure: Analogies sometimes import a different sign or reference-state convention.

Check my setup (don't solve) before

Here is a problem and MY setup for it. Do NOT solve it or compute a number.
Critique only the setup: Is the system/boundary chosen well? Are the right
balances written? Is the model/equation-of-state choice justified? Are the
assumptions stated and admissible? List anything missing or inconsistent.

PROBLEM: <paste problem>
MY SETUP: <paste your system, boundary, assumptions, equations>
Check: If it starts computing a numerical answer, stop it — that is not what you asked for.
Common failure: Models often ignore the instruction and solve the whole problem anyway.

Hint, don't solve during

I want to solve this myself. Give me only the NEXT hint — one step — and
then stop and wait. Do not reveal the full solution or the final answer.
If I am heading in the wrong direction, tell me that without giving the fix.

PROBLEM: <paste problem>
WHERE I AM: <what you have tried>
Check: Ask for the next hint only after attempting the step yourself.
Common failure: Models tend to give the whole solution despite the instruction, especially on later turns.

Sanity / limiting-case check after

Here is my answer and the problem. Do NOT redo my algebra. Instead check
whether the result is physically reasonable: test a limiting case, do a
units check, and tell me if the sign and order of magnitude are what you
would expect. Flag anything suspicious; do not just agree.

PROBLEM: <paste problem>
MY ANSWER: <paste answer with units>
Check: A model that only says "looks right" has not done the check — push for the limiting case and the units.
Common failure: Models (especially eager-to-please ones) rubber-stamp the answer instead of testing it.

Walk me through this answer key during

Here is a problem and the answer key, which skips steps. Walk me through
the reasoning between the steps shown, in this book's notation. Where the
key makes a choice (a model, a reference state, an approximation), name it
and say why.

PROBLEM: <paste>     ANSWER KEY: <paste>
Check: Cross-check the filled-in steps against a worked example in the chapter.
Common failure: Models may invent plausible intermediate steps that do not match the key's actual method.

Connect this to another chapter study

How does the concept below connect to <chapter / topic N>? Make the
connection explicit using this book's notation. If there is no real
connection, say so plainly — do not manufacture one.

CONCEPT: <paste>
Check: Ask it to point to the specific equation or definition that links them.
Common failure: Models invent connections that sound plausible but are not in the material.
